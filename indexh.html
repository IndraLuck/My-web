Q2. Chemically and etymologically, the term "Carbohydrate" literally means:

Correct Answer: B) Hydrated carbon

Explanation: The name originates from "carbon + water" (C + H2O), where each carbon atom is associated with a water molecule. Option A is incorrect because hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen (C + H) without water molecules. Options C and D are inaccurate chemical terms.

Q3. Carbohydrates are water-soluble (hydrophilic), whereas Hydrocarbons are water-insoluble (hydrophobic). Which of the following is classified as a Hydrocarbon?

Correct Answer: C) Fatty acids (Lipids)

Explanation: Fatty acids consist of long carbon and hydrogen chains without water molecules, making them insoluble hydrocarbons. Options A, B, and D are carbohydrates (hydrated carbons) that readily dissolve or interact with water.

Q4. Approximately what percentage of the typical human daily diet consists of carbohydrates?

Correct Answer: C) 60%

Explanation: The professor noted that carbohydrates constitute approximately 60% of human food consumption (such as bread, rice, and pasta). Options A, B, and D do not represent the dietary proportion stated in the lecture.

Q5. Which of the following represents an ANIMAL tissue storage source of carbohydrate?

Correct Answer: C) Glycogen

Explanation: Glycogen is the animal storage form of carbohydrate found in skeletal muscle and liver tissue. Options A and B are plant-derived carbohydrates (starch and cell wall cellulose). Option D is a plant polysaccharide.

Part 2: Chemical Definition & Derivation from Glycerol

Q6. Scientifically, Carbohydrates are defined as aldehyde or ketone derivatives of:

Correct Answer: C) Polyhydric alcohols

Explanation: Carbohydrates are aldehyde or ketone derivatives of polyhydric alcohols (alcohols containing 3 or more hydroxyl -OH groups). Options A (1 -OH) and B (2 -OH) cannot form functional carbohydrates. Option D lacks hydroxyl groups.

Q7. What is the smallest polyhydric alcohol from which the simplest 3-carbon carbohydrates (trioses) are derived?

Correct Answer: C) Glycerol

Explanation: Glycerol contains 3 carbon atoms and 3 hydroxyl groups (3 C + 3 -OH), making it the smallest polyhydric alcohol. Options A (monohydric) and B (dihydric) are smaller non-polyhydric alcohols. Sorbitol is a 6-carbon polyhydric alcohol.

Q8. Oxidation of the PRIMARY alcohol group of Glycerol yields which simplest aldotriose sugar?

Correct Answer: B) Glyceraldehyde

Explanation: Oxidizing the primary alcohol group at C1 of glycerol yields the aldehyde group of Glyceraldehyde (aldotriose). Option A is formed by oxidizing the secondary alcohol group at C2. Option C is a 4-carbon tetrose. Option D is a 6-carbon alcohol.

Q9. Oxidation of the SECONDARY alcohol group of Glycerol yields which simplest ketotriose sugar?

Correct Answer: B) Dihydroxyacetone

Explanation: Oxidizing the middle secondary alcohol group at C2 of glycerol converts it into a ketone group, forming Dihydroxyacetone. Option A is an aldotriose. Options C (pentose) and D (hexose) contain more than 3 carbons.

Q10. Sorbitol is a 6-carbon polyhydric alcohol containing 6 hydroxyl groups. Its oxidation yields which hexose sugar(s)?

Correct Answer: B) Glucose or Fructose

Explanation: Sorbitol is the 6-carbon polyhydric alcohol precursor; oxidation at C1 forms Glucose (aldohexose) while oxidation at C2 forms Fructose (ketohexose). Options A (3C), C (5C), and D (4C) have fewer carbon atoms.

Part 3: General Classifications (Structural & Nutritional)

Q11. Which structural category of carbohydrates cannot be further hydrolyzed into simpler sugar units?

Correct Answer: A) Monosaccharides

Explanation: Monosaccharides consist of a single monomeric sugar unit that cannot be hydrolyzed further. Options B (2 units), C (3–10 units), and D (>10 units) yield free monosaccharides upon hydrolysis.

Q12. Oligosaccharides are structurally defined as carbohydrates yielding how many monosaccharide units upon hydrolysis?

Correct Answer: B) 3 to 10 units

Explanation: Carbohydrates containing 3 to 10 linked monosaccharide units are classified as Oligosaccharides. Option A defines a Disaccharide. Options C and D represent Polysaccharides.

Q13. From a nutritional standpoint, why are Complex Sugars (Polysaccharides) metabolically healthier than Simple Sugars (sweets/pastries)?

Correct Answer: B) They digest slowly, causing a gradual glucose absorption and steady insulin response

Explanation: Complex sugars take time to break down in the intestine, leading to a slow release of glucose into blood and preventing rapid insulin surges. Simple sugars cause immediate blood glucose spikes and rapid insulin surges. Options A, C, and D are physiologically false.

Part 4: Carbon-Count & Functional Group Naming

Q14. What is the general chemical empirical formula for a Monosaccharide hexose (n = 6)?

Correct Answer: B) C6H12O6

Explanation: Using the general monosaccharide formula Cn(H2O)n, for n = 6, the formula becomes C6H12O6. Option D represents a disaccharide (C12H22O11).

Q15. In carbohydrate nomenclature, ketose sugars generally end with the suffix "-ulose". Which of the following ketohexose sugars is the UNIQUE EXCEPTION that does NOT end with "-ulose"?

Correct Answer: D) Fructose (Levulose)

Explanation: As stressed in the lecture, Fructose is a ketohexose that ends with "-ose" rather than "-ulose". Options A, B, and C are ketose sugars that strictly follow the "-ulose" suffix rule.

Q16. Erythrose is an aldotetrose sugar (4 carbons) found physiologically in red blood cells (erythrocytes). What is the name of its corresponding Ketotetrose?

Correct Answer: A) Erythrulose

Explanation: Adding the "-ulose" suffix to the 4-carbon tetrose yields Erythrulose (ketotetrose). Option B is a 5-carbon ketopentose. Option C is a 7-carbon ketoheptose.

Q17. Which of the following is classified as a Ketoheptose (7-carbon ketose sugar) operating as an intermediate in metabolic pathways?

Correct Answer: A) Sedoheptulose

Explanation: Sedoheptulose is a 7-carbon sugar containing a ketone group (indicated by "-ulose") that acts as a metabolic intermediate. Option B is a 3-carbon ketotriose. Options C and D are 5-carbon aldopentoses.

Part 5: Medical Importance of Pentoses (3.5-Mark Section)

Q18. Ribose and Deoxyribose serve a critical physiological role as the structural sugar backbone of:

Correct Answer: B) RNA and DNA

Explanation: Ribose forms the sugar component of RNA, while Deoxyribose forms the sugar component of DNA. Options A, C, and D are polysaccharides composed of glucose or amino sugars.

Q19. High-energy cellular currencies (ATP and GTP) contain Adenosine. What is the exact chemical composition of Adenosine?

Correct Answer: B) Adenine + Ribose

Explanation: Adenosine is formed by attaching the nitrogenous base Adenine to the pentose sugar Ribose. Attachment of phosphate groups yields AMP, ADP, and ATP.

Q20. Ribose enters the structural composition of which vital metabolic coenzymes?

Correct Answer: A) NAD+, NADP+, and FAD

Explanation: Ribose is a structural component of key hydrogen-carrying metabolic coenzymes (NAD+, NADP+, and FAD). Options B, C, and D are vitamins, hormones, and proteins.

Q21. Which two aldopentose sugars attach to proteins to form Glycoproteins in the cell membrane?

Correct Answer: B) Arabinose and Xylose

Explanation: Arabinose and Xylose are pentoses that attach to proteins to synthesize cell membrane glycoproteins. Options A, C, and D do not represent the specific glycoprotein pentose pair named in the lecture.

Q22. Which specialized aldopentose sugar is found specifically in human Cardiac Muscle tissue (Human Heart Muscle)?

Correct Answer: C) Lyxose

Explanation: As emphasized in the 3.5-mark lecture question, Lyxose is an aldopentose present specifically in human cardiac muscle tissue. Options A, B, and D perform other functions.

Q23. Ribose-5-phosphate and Ribulose-5-phosphate serve as vital intermediates in which metabolic pathway?

Correct Answer: B) Hexose Monophosphate Shunt (HMP Shunt / PPP)

Explanation: Ribose-5-P and Ribulose-5-P are essential intermediates generated in the Pentose Phosphate Pathway (HMP shunt). Option A utilizes Xylulose-5-P.

Q24. Xylulose (a ketopentose) serves as an intermediate in which metabolic pathway?

Correct Answer: B) Uronic Acid Pathway

Explanation: Xylulose is a ketopentose intermediate produced in the Uronic Acid pathway. Option A utilizes ribose/ribulose phosphates.

Part 6: Hexoses Metabolism, Tissue Conversions, & Lactose Intolerance

Q25. Why is Glucose clinically designated as the "Blood Sugar"?

Correct Answer: B) It is the only monosaccharide that circulates freely in human blood to provide tissue energy

Explanation: Routine clinical blood sugar tests measure glucose because it is the primary circulating energy monosaccharide in systemic blood.

Q26. All non-glucose monosaccharides (Fructose, Galactose, Mannose) absorbed from the intestine travel via portal circulation to which organ for enzymatic conversion into Glucose?

Correct Answer: C) Liver

Explanation: The liver absorbs non-glucose monosaccharides from portal blood and enzymatically converts them into Glucose before releasing them into the systemic circulation.

Q27. In male Seminal Vesicles, circulating Glucose is enzymatically converted into which sugar to nourish spermatozoa in seminal fluid?

Correct Answer: B) Fructose

Explanation: Seminal vesicles convert glucose into Fructose, which is secreted into seminal fluid to nourish sperm. Option A occurs in lactating mammary glands.

Q28. During lactation in female Mammary Glands, Glucose is converted into Galactose, which then condenses with Glucose to synthesize:

Correct Answer: B) Lactose (Milk Sugar)

Explanation: Mammary glands convert glucose into galactose and condense them to produce Lactose (breast milk sugar). Option A is table sugar (Glucose + Fructose).

Q29. What is the physiological cause of watery diarrhea, colic, and abdominal distension in infants with Lactose Intolerance?

Correct Answer: B) Deficiency of intestinal Lactase, leading to bacterial fermentation and osmotic fluid influx

Explanation: Lack of intestinal Lactase leaves undigested lactose in the gut lumen, causing bacterial fermentation (gas) and an osmotic pull that draws water into the intestine, triggering watery diarrhea.

Q30. What is the clinical dietary management for an infant diagnosed with Lactose Intolerance?

Correct Answer: B) Prescribe commercial LF (Lactose-Free) formula containing pre-hydrolyzed glucose/fructose

Explanation: Natural milk must be replaced with commercial LF (Lactose-Free) infant formulas that supply pre-hydrolyzed simple sugars.

Part 7: Ring Cyclization Mechanisms, Chemical Proofs, & Anomers

Q31. Which of the following chemical experimental findings proved that Glucose in solution exists as a cyclic ring rather than an open chain?

Correct Answer: B) Glucose fails to react with Schiff's reagent and fails to form a bisulfite adduct with NaHSO3

Explanation: Open-chain aldehydes react with Schiff's reagent and sodium hydrogen sulfite; glucose's failure to undergo these reactions proves its C1 aldehyde group is tied up in a cyclic ring.

Q32. During glucose cyclization in water, hydration across the C1 carbonyl double bond forms an unstable intermediate termed:

Correct Answer: A) Aldenol (geminal diol)

Explanation: Adding water across the C=O double bond at C1 forms an unstable intermediate containing two -OH groups on C1, called an Aldenol.

Q33. Intramolecular condensation (water removal) between Carbon-1 and the -OH group at Carbon-5 of Glucose produces which ring structure?

Correct Answer: B) 6-membered Pyranose ring

Explanation: Condensation between C1 and C5 forms a 6-membered ring (5 carbons + 1 ring oxygen) named Glucopyranose. Condensation between C1 and C4 yields a 5-membered Furanose ring.

Q34. Cyclization of Fructose involves reaction between its carbonyl carbon at Carbon-2 and Carbon-5, yielding a 5-membered ring named:

Correct Answer: B) Fructofuranose

Explanation: Fructose is a ketose with its carbonyl group at C2; linkage between C2 and C5 forms a 5-membered Fructofuranose ring. Linkage between C2 and C6 forms a 6-membered Fructopyranose ring.

Q35. What is the definition of the Anomeric Carbon atom?

Correct Answer: B) The former carbonyl carbon converted from symmetric (achiral) to asymmetric (chiral) upon ring closure

Explanation: Upon cyclization, the achiral carbonyl carbon (C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses) becomes bonded to 4 different groups, creating a new chiral center termed the Anomeric Carbon.

Q36. Where is the Anomeric Carbon located in Aldoses versus Ketoses?

Correct Answer: A) C1 in Aldoses; C2 in Ketoses

Explanation: Aldoses contain their aldehyde group at C1, making C1 the anomeric carbon. Ketoses contain their ketone group at C2, making C2 the anomeric carbon.

Part 8: Haworth & Chair Conformational Rules

Q37. According to Haworth ring projection rules, a hydroxyl group (-OH) located on the RIGHT side of a Fischer open-chain projection is drawn:

Correct Answer: B) Downward below the ring plane

Explanation: Haworth Rule 1: Groups on the RIGHT in Fischer are drawn DOWNWARD in Haworth. Groups on the LEFT in Fischer are drawn UPWARD in Haworth.

Q38. In Haworth projections of D-hexoses, what is the EXCEPTION rule regarding the terminal -CH2OH group at Carbon-6?

Correct Answer: B) It is drawn UPWARD above the ring plane regardless of its open-chain side

Explanation: As highlighted by the professor, C6 (-CH2OH) in D-sugars is the exception to the right/down rule and is always drawn UPWARD above the ring at C5.

Q39. In the Chair conformation of sugars, how are the α and β anomers distinguished by hydroxyl group positioning?

Correct Answer: B) α = Axial (perpendicular); β = Equatorial (slanted/parallel)

Explanation: In chair conformation, an axial (perpendicular) anomeric -OH represents the α-anomer (right/down), while an equatorial (slanted) -OH represents the β-anomer (left/up).

Part 9: Stereoisomer Calculations (2n Rule) & Optical Activity

Q40. The total number of stereoisomers for any carbohydrate is calculated using the formula 2n. What does "n" represent?

Correct Answer: C) Number of chiral (asymmetric) carbon atoms

Explanation: The 2n rule calculates total stereoisomers, where n is the count of chiral carbons (carbons bonded to 4 different groups).

Q41. Dihydroxyacetone (C3H6O3) contains how many chiral carbon atoms, and how many optical stereoisomers does it possess?

Correct Answer: B) 0 chiral carbons; ZERO optical stereoisomers (20 = 1 structural form)

Explanation: C1 and C3 of dihydroxyacetone have 2 identical hydrogens, and C2 is a carbonyl carbon; thus n = 0, yielding 20 = 1 structure with zero optical isomers.

Q42. Open-chain Glucose contains 4 chiral carbons (at C2, C3, C4, C5). How many total stereoisomers does Glucose possess?

Correct Answer: C) 16 stereoisomers (24)

Explanation: For open-chain glucose, n = 4; applying 2n = 24 = 16 stereoisomers.

Q43. Glucose rotates plane-polarized light to the RIGHT (+52.5°), giving it which commercial name?

Correct Answer: B) Dextrose (Dextrorotatory, d/+)

Explanation: Glucose rotates light to the right (dextrorotatory, +52.5°), earning the commercial name Dextrose. Fructose rotates light to the left, earning the name Levulose.

Q44. Fructose rotates plane-polarized light to the LEFT (-91°), giving it which commercial name?

Correct Answer: B) Levulose (Levorotatory, l/-)

Explanation: Fructose is strongly levorotatory (-91°), earning the commercial/pharmaceutical name Levulose.

Q45. What are the standard experimental conditions required to measure the Specific Rotation (α) of a substance in a polarimeter?

Correct Answer: B) 100 grams of solute in 100 mL solvent; polarimeter tube length of 10 cm (1 decimeter)

Explanation: Specific rotation (α) is standardized at a concentration of 100g solute/100mL solvent in a 10 cm (1 dm) path length tube.

Q46. A Racemic Mixture contains equimolar amounts of dextrorotatory (d/+) and levorotatory (l/-) isomers of the same substance. Why is a Racemic Mixture OPTICALLY INACTIVE (net rotation = 0°)?

Correct Answer: B) Equal and opposite optical rotations cancel each other out (+52.5° + -52.5° = 0°)

Explanation: In a racemic mixture, the rightward rotation of the d-isomer is exactly counteracted by the leftward rotation of the l-isomer, resulting in zero net rotation.

Q47. The chemical or physical separation process used to isolate pure d(+) and l(-) optical isomers from a racemic mixture is termed:

Correct Answer: B) Resolution

Explanation: Resolution is the process of resolving/separating a racemic mixture into its active individual enantiomers, restoring optical activity to each separated fraction.

Part 1: Stereoisomers, Chiral Carbons, & The 2n Rule

Q1. What is the fundamental structural requirement for a carbohydrate molecule to exhibit optical isomerism (stereoisomerism)?

Correct Answer: B) Presence of at least one asymmetric (chiral) carbon atom

Explanation: A molecule can exist in multiple stereoisomeric forms only if it possesses at least one chiral (asymmetric) carbon atom—a carbon atom bonded to four completely different atoms or functional groups. Options A, C, and D are specific structural features that do not define optical stereoisomerism.

Q2. In carbohydrate notation, how is optical activity (direction of plane-polarized light rotation) distinguished from spatial stereoisomeric configuration?

Correct Answer: B) Optical activity uses Small d/l or (+)/(-); configuration uses Capital D/L

Explanation: Small d (or +) denotes Dextrorotatory (rightward light rotation) and Small l (or -) denotes Levorotatory (leftward light rotation). Capital D/L denotes the spatial configuration of the hydroxyl group on the penultimate chiral carbon. Capital D/L configuration and small d/l optical activity are completely independent properties.

Q3. According to the 2n rule for calculating total stereoisomers, how many chiral carbon atoms and optical stereoisomers does Dihydroxyacetone possess?

Correct Answer: B) 0 chiral carbons; 1 structural form (0 optical stereoisomers)

Explanation: In Dihydroxyacetone (C3H6O3), Carbon-1 and Carbon-3 each hold two identical hydrogen atoms, and Carbon-2 forms a double bond with oxygen. Because it lacks chiral carbons (n = 0), applying 20 = 1 yields a single structural form with zero optical stereoisomers.

Q4. Glyceraldehyde (C3H6O3) contains 1 chiral carbon atom. What is its total number of stereoisomers, and what are their spatial forms?

Correct Answer: B) 2 stereoisomers; D-Glyceraldehyde and L-Glyceraldehyde

Explanation: Glyceraldehyde possesses 1 chiral carbon at Carbon-2 (n = 1). Applying 21 = 2 yields exactly 2 optical stereoisomers: D-Glyceraldehyde (hydroxyl on the right) and L-Glyceraldehyde (hydroxyl on the left).

Q5. Open-chain D-Glucose contains 4 chiral carbon atoms (at C2, C3, C4, and C5). What is the total number of stereoisomers for Glucose, and how many of them are true ENANTIOMERS (mirror images) of D-Glucose?

Correct Answer: A) 16 total stereoisomers; 2 are Enantiomers (D-Glucose and L-Glucose)

Explanation: Applying 2n where n = 4 yields 24 = 16 total stereoisomers. Out of these 16 stereoisomers, only 2 molecules are true Enantiomers (non-superimposable mirror images of each other: D-Glucose and L-Glucose). The remaining 14 stereoisomers are diastereomers (such as D-Galactose, D-Mannose, etc.).

Q6. Which carbohydrate molecule is universally designated as the "Reference Sugar" for stereoisomerism and configuration systems?

Correct Answer: B) D-Glyceraldehyde

Explanation: D-Glyceraldehyde is the simplest aldose containing a chiral carbon, making it the fundamental reference sugar from which all D- and L-carbohydrate configurations are structurally derived. Dihydroxyacetone lacks chiral carbons and cannot serve as a reference.

Q7. What is the spatial configuration of the vast majority of naturally occurring carbohydrates present in human body tissues?

Correct Answer: B) D-configuration

Explanation: The vast majority of naturally occurring carbohydrates in human and mammalian tissues exist in the D-configuration (where the hydroxyl group on the penultimate chiral carbon is on the right).

Part 2: Classification of Isomers (Enantiomers, Diastereomers, Anomers, & Epimers)

Q8. How are Constitutional (Structural) Isomers defined, and which pair represents an example of constitutional isomers?

Correct Answer: B) Molecules with the same molecular formula but different functional group connectivity; Glyceraldehyde and Dihydroxyacetone

Explanation: Constitutional isomers share the exact same empirical molecular formula (C3H6O3) but differ in the sequence and connectivity of atoms/functional groups (Aldotriose vs. Ketotriose). D-Glucose and L-Glucose are Enantiomers. D-Glucose and D-Galactose are Diastereomers/Epimers.

Q9. Stereoisomers that are non-superimposable MIRROR IMAGES of each other are classified as:

Correct Answer: C) Enantiomers

Explanation: By definition, stereoisomers that form non-superimposable mirror images of each other are called Enantiomers (e.g., D-Glyceraldehyde and L-Glyceraldehyde, D-Glucose and L-Glucose).

Q10. What are Anomers, and which specific structural carbon atom defines anomeric isomerism?

Correct Answer: B) Diastereomers differing in -OH configuration at the Anomeric Carbon (C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses); α- and β-forms

Explanation: Anomers are diastereomers created upon ring cyclization, differing specifically in the spatial orientation of the hydroxyl group attached to the former carbonyl (anomeric) carbon (C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses), forming α- and β-anomers.

Q11. Epimers are defined as diastereomers that differ in the spatial configuration of a hydroxyl group at:

Correct Answer: B) Exactly ONE single chiral carbon atom OTHER than the anomeric carbon

Explanation: Epimers are diastereomers that differ in the orientation of the -OH group at exactly one chiral carbon atom, excluding the anomeric carbon. If they differ at the anomeric carbon, they are Anomers.

Q12. D-Mannose is the C2-epimer of D-Glucose. How does D-Mannose structurally differ from D-Glucose?

Correct Answer: A) -OH group at C2 is on the Left in D-Mannose, but on the Right in D-Glucose

Explanation: D-Mannose differs from D-Glucose at Carbon-2 ONLY (C2-epimer); its C2 hydroxyl group projects to the left, whereas in D-Glucose it projects to the right.

Q13. D-Galactose is the C4-epimer of D-Glucose. How does D-Galactose structurally differ from D-Glucose?

Correct Answer: B) -OH group at C4 is on the Left in D-Galactose, but on the Right in D-Glucose

Explanation: D-Galactose differs from D-Glucose at Carbon-4 ONLY (C4-epimer); its C4 hydroxyl group projects to the left, whereas in D-Glucose it projects to the right.

Q14. (TRICKY EXAM FAVORITE): Are D-Galactose and D-Mannose epimers of each other? Why or why not?

Correct Answer: B) NO, because they differ in -OH configuration at TWO chiral carbons (C2 AND C4) simultaneously

Explanation: By definition, epimers must differ at ONLY ONE single chiral carbon. D-Mannose differs at C2, and D-Galactose differs at C4; when compared directly to each other, they differ at two carbon centers (C2 AND C4) simultaneously, making them standard diastereomers, NOT epimers of each other.

Part 3: Monosaccharide Derivatives & Clinical Applications

Q15. Oxidation of Carbon-1 (aldehyde group) of D-Glucose yields which sugar acid derivative?

Correct Answer: B) Gluconic Acid (Aldonic acid)

Explanation: Oxidation of the C1 aldehyde group converts it into a carboxyl group, forming an Aldonic acid (Gluconic Acid in glucose). Oxidation of C6 forms Uronic acid. Oxidation of both C1 and C6 forms Aldaric acid.

Q16. Oxidation of Carbon-6 (primary alcohol group) of D-Glucose yields which clinically important sugar acid derivative?

Correct Answer: B) Glucuronic Acid (Uronic acid)

Explanation: Oxidation of the C6 primary alcohol group while keeping the C1 aldehyde intact produces a Uronic acid (Glucuronic Acid).

Q17. Oxidation of BOTH Carbon-1 AND Carbon-6 of D-Glucose simultaneously yields which dicarboxylic sugar acid?

Correct Answer: C) Glucaric Acid (Saccharic/Aldaric acid)

Explanation: Oxidizing both the C1 aldehyde and C6 primary alcohol groups yields a dicarboxylic sugar acid termed an Aldaric acid (Glucaric Acid).

Q18. Chemical reduction of D-Glucose yields which sugar alcohol (polyol)?

Correct Answer: B) Sorbitol (Glucitol)

Explanation: Reducing the aldehyde group of D-Glucose yields the 6-carbon sugar alcohol Sorbitol. Reducing D-Mannose yields Mannitol.

Q19. Chemical reduction of D-Fructose yields WHICH sugar alcohol product(s)?

Correct Answer: C) A mixture of TWO sugar alcohols: Sorbitol AND Mannitol

Explanation: Fructose is a ketohexose with its carbonyl group at C2. Reducing C2 creates a new chiral carbon center, projecting the new -OH group to the right (forming Sorbitol) or to the left (forming Mannitol).

Q20. (CLINICAL PHARMACY APPLICATION): Mannitol solution is administered intravenously as an Osmotic Diuretic in clinical practice to treat:

Correct Answer: B) Increased Intracranial Pressure (cerebral edema) and Intraocular Pressure (glaucoma)

Explanation: Mannitol acts as an Osmotic Diuretic; it remains in the circulation and draws excess fluid out of brain tissue or the eye into blood to be excreted in urine, preventing brain compression during trauma/surgery or treating glaucoma.

Q21. (EXAM FAVORITE): L-Fucose is a specialized Deoxy Sugar found in biological cell membranes. Chemically, L-Fucose is defined as:

Correct Answer: B) 6-Deoxy-L-galactose

Explanation: As highlighted by the professor, L-Fucose is a unique deoxy sugar formed by removing the oxygen atom from Carbon-6 of L-galactose (6-deoxy-L-galactose). 2-Deoxyribose is the sugar in DNA.

Q22. Amino Sugars are formed by replacing the hydroxyl group (-OH) at Carbon-2 of a monosaccharide with an Amino group (-NH2). Examples include:

Correct Answer: A) Glucosamine, Galactosamine, and Mannosamine

Explanation: Replacing the C2 hydroxyl group with an amino group yields amino sugars such as Glucosamine, Galactosamine, and Mannosamine.

Q23. Condensation of Mannosamine with Pyruvic Acid yields Neuraminic Acid. Subsequent N-acetylation produces N-Acetylneuraminic Acid (NANA). NANA is classified as a:

Correct Answer: C) Amino Sugar Acid (Sialic Acid)

Explanation: N-Acetylneuraminic acid (NANA), also termed Sialic Acid, is structurally classified as an Amino Sugar Acid formed by condensing mannosamine with pyruvic acid followed by N-acetylation.

Part 4: Disaccharides, Glycosidic Bonds, & Invert Sugar

Q24. What structural requirement is ABSOLUTELY MANDATORY for a bond to be classified as a Glycosidic Bond in carbohydrates?

Correct Answer: B) It MUST involve the Anomeric Hydroxyl Group (C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses) of at least one sugar

Explanation: A glycosidic bond requires the participation of the reactive anomeric hydroxyl group (C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses). If a bond is formed between non-anomeric carbons, it is not a glycosidic bond.

Q25. Which type of glycosidic bond links nitrogenous bases to ribose/deoxyribose sugars in Nucleosides and Nucleotides?

Correct Answer: B) N-glycosidic bond

Explanation: Linkage between the anomeric carbon of a sugar and an amine nitrogen of a purine or pyrimidine base forms an N-glycosidic bond. O-glycosidic bonds link sugar-to-sugar -OH groups. S-glycosidic bonds involve sulfhydryl groups of proteins.

Q26. Maltose (Malt sugar) is a disaccharide derived from starch digestion. What is its exact monosaccharide composition and glycosidic linkage?

Correct Answer: A) 2 α-D-Glucose units linked by an α(1 → 4) glycosidic bond

Explanation: Maltose consists of two α-D-glucose units joined by an α(1 → 4) glycosidic bond. Option B describes Isomaltose. Option C describes Cellobiose. Option D describes Sucrose.

Q27. Isomaltose is derived from starch digestion at branch points. What is its exact glycosidic linkage?

Correct Answer: B) α(1 → 6)

Explanation: Isomaltose consists of two α-D-glucose units joined by an α(1 → 6) glycosidic linkage formed at starch branch points.

Q28. Trehalose is a disaccharide found in fungi and yeast used as a sweetener and preservative. What is its linkage, reducing property, and anomeric availability?

Correct Answer: B) α(1 → 1) linkage; NON-REDUCING sugar; Has NO α/β anomers

Explanation: Trehalose links two α-D-glucose units via their anomeric C1 carbons (α1 → 1). Because both anomeric carbons are tied up in the bond, there is no free carbonyl group, making it Non-Reducing with no optical anomers.

Q29. Cellobiose is the disaccharide intermediate obtained from partial hydrolysis of Cellulose. What is its structural composition and linkage?

Correct Answer: B) 2 β-D-Glucose units linked by a β(1 → 4) glycosidic bond

Explanation: Cellobiose consists of two β-D-glucose units joined by a β(1 → 4) glycosidic bond.

Q30. Lactose (Milk sugar) consists of β-D-Galactose and β-D-Glucose linked by a β(1 → 4) galactosidic bond. Why is Lactose classified as a REDUCING sugar with active α and β anomers?

Correct Answer: B) The anomeric carbon of Galactose (C1) is bound, but the anomeric carbon of Glucose (C1) remains FREE

Explanation: In lactose (β1 → 4), galactose contributes its anomeric C1, but the second sugar (glucose) has its anomeric C1 hydroxyl completely free, conferring reducing properties and allowing α/β anomeric forms.

Q31. Sucrose (Table sugar) is composed of α-D-Glucose and β-D-Fructose joined by an α1 → β2 glycosidic bond. Why is Sucrose classified as a NON-REDUCING sugar?

Correct Answer: B) The anomeric carbon of Glucose (C1) AND the anomeric carbon of Fructose (C2) are BOTH tied up in the glycosidic bond

Explanation: In sucrose, the bond is formed between C1 of Glucose and C2 of Fructose (α1 → β2). Since both functional anomeric carbons are mutually bound, there is no free anomeric carbon, rendering Sucrose Non-Reducing with no α/β anomers.

Q32. Pure Sucrose solution is Dextrorotatory (+66.5°). Upon hydrolysis, it releases free D-Glucose (+52.5°) and free D-Fructose (-91°). Why is the hydrolyzed mixture named "INVERT SUGAR"?

Correct Answer: B) Because the net optical rotation inverts from Dextrorotatory (+66.5°) to Levorotatory (-38.5°) due to the strong levorotatory power of free Fructose

Explanation: Free Fructose is strongly levorotatory (-91°), which exceeds the dextrorotatory rotation of free Glucose (+52.5°). The overall net optical rotation of the solution inverts from +66.5° (Right) to -38.5° (Left); hence it is termed Invert Sugar (e.g., natural honey).

Part 5: Polysaccharides & Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)

Q33. What is the structural definition of a Homopolysaccharide versus a Heteropolysaccharide?

Correct Answer: A) Homopolysaccharide = repeating single monosaccharide type; Heteropolysaccharide = multiple different sugar types

Explanation: Homopolysaccharides consist of a single repeating monosaccharide monomer (e.g., glucose in starch, glycogen, cellulose). Heteropolysaccharides consist of two or more different monosaccharide types (e.g., GAGs).

Q34. Starch consists of two structural layers: Amylose (inner) and Amylopectin (outer). Compare their structural percentages, linkages, and branching.

Correct Answer: B) Amylose (15–20% of starch, linear unbranched α1 → 4 bonds); Amylopectin (80–85% of starch, branched α1 → 4 main chain with α1 → 6 branch points)

Explanation: Amylose represents the inner 15–20% of starch as a linear unbranched tape of α(1 → 4) glucose units. Amylopectin represents the outer 80–85% as a branched network with α(1 → 4) main chains and α(1 → 6) branch points every 24–30 glucose units.

Q35. Glycogen (Animal Starch) is stored in Liver and Skeletal Muscle tissue. How does its branching structure compare to plant Amylopectin?

Correct Answer: B) Glycogen is more highly branched, with α(1 → 6) branch points occurring every 12 to 14 glucose units

Explanation: Glycogen resembles amylopectin in having α(1 → 4) main chains and α(1 → 6) branches, but it is much more densely branched (every 12–14 glucose units compared to 24–30 in amylopectin).

Q36. (PHYSIOLOGICAL CONTRAST): Why does Liver Glycogen maintain systemic blood glucose levels during fasting, whereas Muscle Glycogen CANNOT release glucose into the blood?

Correct Answer: B) Liver tissue possesses Glucose-6-Phosphatase to release free Glucose into blood; Muscle tissue lacks Glucose-6-Phosphatase, trapping glucose for local contraction energy

Explanation: The liver is "generous" and contains Glucose-6-Phosphatase, allowing glycogenolysis to supply free glucose to systemic blood. Muscle is "selfish" and lacks this enzyme, trapping glucose inside muscle cells exclusively for contraction energy.

Q37. Cellulose is a plant structural cell wall homopolysaccharide composed of β-D-Glucose units linked by β(1 → 4) glycosidic bonds. Why is Cellulose indigestible by human digestive enzymes?

Correct Answer: B) Because human digestive secretions lack enzymes capable of hydrolyzing β(1 → 4) glycosidic bonds

Explanation: Human digestive enzymes (like α-amylase) can hydrolyze only α-glycosidic bonds (α1,4 and α1,6); they lack β-glucosidase enzymes to cleave β(1 → 4) bonds of cellulose.

Q41. Explain the "Sponge / Resilience Mechanism" of Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) in joint synovial fluid.

Correct Answer: B) High negative charges (-COO- and -SO42-) cause repulsion, spreading chains to trap enormous amounts of water; joint compression squeezes water out, and release re-absorbs water for shock absorption

Explanation: High density of negative charges causes mutual repulsion between GAG chains, creating open spaces that trap water like a sponge. Under mechanical compression, water is squeezed out to cushion joint impact; when pressure is released, water immediately re-enters, providing resilience and shock absorption.

Q40. How does an amino acid behave in an ALKALINE MEDIUM (High pH / low H+ ions), and toward which electrode does it migrate during electrophoresis?

Part 10: Isoelectric Point (pI) Calculation Formulas & Solved Problems

Q41. What is the definition of the Isoelectric Point (pI / Isoelectric pH)?

Correct Answer: B) The exact pH at which an amino acid carries NO NET ELECTRICAL CHARGE (zwitterion form) and does NOT migrate toward Anode or Cathode in an electric field

Explanation: At the Isoelectric Point (pI), positive and negative charges on the amino acid are perfectly balanced (net charge = 0), preventing directional migration in an electrophoretic field.

Q42. What is the mathematical formula for calculating the Isoelectric Point (pI) of a NEUTRAL Amino Acid (e.g., Glycine, Alanine)?

Correct Answer: B) pI = pKa1 (α-COOH) + pKa2 (α-NH3+)2

Explanation: For a neutral monoamino monocarboxylic amino acid, pI is the simple arithmetic average of the α-carboxyl pKa (pKa1) and α-amino pKa (pKa2).

Q43. (CALCULATION PROBLEM 1): Calculate the Isoelectric Point (pI) of Glycine given that its pKa1 (α-COOH) = 2.34 and its pKa2 (α-NH3+) = 9.60.

Correct Answer: C) 5.97

Explanation: Step-by-step solution:
  1. Identify classification: Glycine is a neutral amino acid.
  2. Apply formula: pI = pKa1 + pKa22 = 2.34 + 9.602 = 11.942 = 5.97.

Q44. What is the rule/formula for calculating the Isoelectric Point (pI) of an ACIDIC Amino Acid (Dicarboxylic, e.g., Aspartic Acid, Glutamic Acid)?

Correct Answer: B) Average of the TWO ACIDIC carboxyl pKa values: pI = pKa1 (α-COOH) + pKaR (side-chain -COOH)2 (ignoring amino pKa)

Explanation: For an acidic amino acid, pI lies between its two acidic carboxyl dissociation constants; thus, calculate the average of pKa1 (α-COOH) and pKaR (side-chain -COOH), completely ignoring the basic amino pKa (pKa2).

Q45. (CALCULATION PROBLEM 2): Calculate the Isoelectric Point (pI) of Aspartic Acid given: pKa1 (α-COOH) = 1.99, pKa2 (α-NH3+) = 9.90, and pKaR (side-chain -COOH) = 3.90.

Correct Answer: C) 2.95

Explanation: Step-by-step solution:
  1. Identify classification: Aspartic acid is an acidic amino acid.
  2. Select the TWO acidic carboxyl pKa values (1.99 and 3.90) and ignore the amino pKa (9.90).
  3. Apply formula: pI = 1.99 + 3.902 = 5.892 = 2.945 ≈ 2.95.

Q46. What is the rule/formula for calculating the Isoelectric Point (pI) of a BASIC Amino Acid (Diamino, e.g., Lysine, Arginine)?

Correct Answer: B) Average of the TWO BASIC amino pKa values: pI = pKa2 (α-NH3+) + pKaR (side-chain amino)2 (ignoring carboxyl pKa)

Explanation: For a basic amino acid, pI lies between its two basic amino dissociation constants; calculate the average of pKa2 (α-NH3+) and pKaR (side-chain amino), ignoring pKa1 (α-COOH).

Q47. (CALCULATION PROBLEM 3): Calculate the Isoelectric Point (pI) of a basic amino acid given: pKa1 (α-COOH) = 2.19, pKa2 (α-NH3+) = 8.85, and pKaR (side-chain amino) = 10.50.

Explanation: Step-by-step solution:

  2. Select the TWO basic amino pKa values (8.85 and 10.50) and ignore the carboxyl pKa (2.19).

Q48. Which chemical reactions are attributed specifically to the Carboxyl group (-COOH) of an amino acid?

Correct Answer: A) Salt formation with alkalis, Decarboxylation (-CO2 removal), and Esterification with alcohols

Explanation: The acidic -COOH group reacts with alkalis to form salts, undergoes esterification with alcohols, and undergoes decarboxylation (loss of CO2) to produce primary amines.

Q49. Decarboxylation of Histidine (removal of CO2 from its carboxyl group) yields which clinically active primary amine involved in allergic reactions and gastric secretion?

Correct Answer: B) Histamine

Explanation: Decarboxylating the carboxyl group of Histidine eliminates CO2, forming Histamine, a potent chemical mediator of inflammatory, allergic, and gastric acid responses.

Q50. Which chemical reactions are attributed specifically to the Amino group (-NH2) of an amino acid?
